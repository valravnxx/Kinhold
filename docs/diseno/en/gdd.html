1. Vision, pillars and era

DECIDEDMedieval survival and building game, 100% in the browser. A single online world: play alone, as a pair or in a group within the same world, with PvP.

DECIDEDAn era with progression: you start in the Early Middle Ages and technology advances up to the Late Middle Ages. No fantasy for now.

Pillars

  • Realistic mechanics. What you would do in real life costs what it would cost.
  • The world is huge and travel matters.
  • From survival to kingdom, or to living well in isolation.
  • The solo player has a complete game.
  • Everything rests on knowledge, skill and equipment, not on character levels or stats.
  • Skill-based combat, without health bars.
  • What is yours is safe while you are away.

2. Platform, camera and product

DECIDEDOwn website with accounts. Paid server if needed. PC, mobile and tablet.

DECIDEDSemi-isometric third person with free rotation.

DECIDEDLanguages: English, Spanish and Russian.

DECIDEDFree with cosmetic-only premium content: unique skins and furniture.

DECIDEDOptional tutorial always available in the pause menu: you can read about every system in the game.

DECIDEDCheaters: immediate ban, also by IP.

DECIDEDMinimum requirements: to be decided midway through development.

METHOD
  • Mouse and keyboard controls and touch controls from the start.
  • Game texts in translation files from the first milestone, so adding languages does not require touching the code.
  • IP bans: note that an IP is sometimes shared by several people (a household, a residence hall, some mobile carriers) and that a VPN gets around it. Best combined with banning the account and its email.

DECIDEDName: Kinhold. Domain and trademark registration still to be checked.

3. Visual style and sound

DECIDEDCode-generated low-poly 3D, with your reviews to fine-tune it.

DECIDEDSound and music that are relaxing and not harsh.

DECIDEDWorld style: «miniature realism» (style test v5): clean shapes, textures made by code, soft light with contact shadows, an uncropped world.

DECIDEDCharacters: never sculpted or invented by code. The body comes from the free MakeHuman mesh (CC0) and in the own workshop its clothing, hair, beard, skin and eyes are made by code. The animations are Quaternius's (CC0), transferred to that skeleton.

METHOD
  • Models defined by parameters (proportions, colours, sides): your adjustments are applied by changing numbers.
  • Screenshots of every new model in each delivery for you to review.
  • One palette per material so everything is consistent.
  • Own headless workshop (Blender + MPFB2, with pinned versions): generates the dressed characters, reduces meshes and transfers animations from one skeleton to another.
  • Levels of detail: simpler versions of characters and pieces to see them from afar and cope with many people on mobile.

4. World and size

DECIDEDAn invented pseudo-Europe, 5 countries with their own style (architecture, clothing, names). Biomes larger than 100 km² in northern, temperate and southern bands. A coherent sea.

DECIDEDA 2 h day and a 1 h night. One season per real month.

DECIDEDEach country has its own resources to trade, but none are essential.

Size calculation

Assumptions: walking at about 5 km/h, a horse at a sustained trot at about 12 km/h, and the sea adds around 40% to the side of the map. One day-night cycle is 3 real hours.

DECIDEDWe start with the medium size: countries of about 60 × 60 km, a world of about 188 × 188 km including the sea. Crossing a country takes about 12 hours on foot or 5 on horseback.

METHOD

Since the world comes from a seed, it can be expanded at the edges later without touching what already exists. With few people at first, the medium world may feel empty: we will keep an eye on it once there are players.

Relief, rivers and forests at real scale

DECIDEDEverything at real scale, like a world still unexplored by man. There are plains, but the world does not feel like a giant flat: mountain ranges like the Pyrenees, with peaks of about 3,000 m, valleys about 1,000 m deep and passes to cross them; the plains lie in the low basins. From anywhere you can see relief on the horizon.

DECIDEDRivers of three sizes, depending on the water they gather: streams 1 to 3 m wide, which can be forded; rivers 10 to 30 m wide, with few fords; great rivers 50 to 150 m wide, which need a boat or a bridge.

DECIDEDTruly dense forests, with clearings. No paths at first: they form through use.

DECIDEDRelief matters: steep slopes slow you down when walking, carts cannot climb steep hills and on a hillside you have to level the ground to lay foundations.

DECIDEDSettling makes sense in specific places without forcing anyone: by the river there is water for thirst, fertile soil in the floodplain, current for the mill, and fords and bridges that can be controlled (and tolled).

METHOD
  • Water first, then the terrain (like the most believable terrain generators, based on hydrology): the mountain ranges and the river network flowing from them to the sea are laid out first, like a tree; the terrain is carved from that network, so valleys appear where water runs and passes where two valleys meet at the top.
  • Each river's width comes from the water it gathers (how many tributaries reach it).
  • Forests with repeated single-mesh trees and simple versions at a distance, in chunks around the player, so they hold up on mobile.

Water and swimming

DECIDEDAt fords you cross on foot. In deep water you swim slowly and it tires you a lot. You cannot swim in armour or heavily loaded: either you drop weight or you don't go in. In great rivers, the current drags you downstream.

DECIDEDCarts only cross at fords and bridges. That way fords and bridges are truly valuable.

METHOD
  • Continent outline first; climate by latitude and sea; large biomes with gradual transitions; natural borders.
  • Seed-based generation, in chunks, saving only the changes.

5. Travel, map and sailing

DECIDEDThe map is discovered by exploring. Endgame cartography: a cartographer's table to draw what has been explored on paper; the map is an item that can be copied, sold or stolen from you.

DECIDEDPaths form through use. Buildable bridges in various materials and shapes.

DECIDEDSailing in a late phase: design ships and sail with others.

METHOD
  • Each ground tile stores how much it is walked on: trail, then road; without use, the grass returns.
  • Bridges and ships use the building system; ships with buoyancy rules.

6. Character, death and logging out

DECIDEDCreation: a few appearance settings, man or woman, and starting country. You start at a random accessible spot in the chosen country (never in the sea or on a peak).

DECIDEDNeeds: hunger, thirst, cold, heat and fatigue, with warnings in circles on the right; tapping them shows the effect and information.

DECIDEDLater on (an expansion after the first version of the survival milestone, not before): five more states, with their warning in the same circles. Wet (rain, swimming or crossing rivers; adds to cold), infected wound (if it isn't cleaned or stitched properly), spoiled food (raw or rotten meat; makes you ill), pain (after a fight or a fall, clumsier until healed) and drunk (beer and wine, with a downside in combat). Boredom, sadness or panic are not included: with the character asleep while offline they would penalise being away, and panic would go against skill-based combat.

DECIDEDThe character does not improve through levels or stats: everything depends on the player's knowledge, skill and equipment.

DECIDEDDeath: you respawn in your bound bed; without a bed, where you started the game. On death, roughly half of what you carry can be looted and the other half breaks. Permadeath is left for a future version.

DECIDEDLogging out: the character falls asleep in the world, goes to their bed automatically if not already in it, and shows a disconnected symbol above them.

DECIDEDWhat breaks on death is always chosen at random, to prevent exploits.

Can a sleeping character be attacked outside their lands?

DECIDED (provisional)Attackable for a few minutes, then not.

  • Inside your lands (or your family's): the sleeping character is protected like your base, with offline protection.
  • Outside your lands: when you log out, the character falls asleep and stays attackable where they are for a few minutes (for example, 5). After that time, if they have a bed they move to it; if not, they stay where they were, but nothing can be done to them any more.
  • If you were in combat in the last minute before logging out, the vulnerable time is longer (for example, 15 minutes).
  • The times are numbers to tune when we test.

7. Equipment, clothing and inventory

DECIDEDEquipment matters and everything is craftable. You start with little more than what you wear: a basic linen shirt and trousers.

DECIDEDCarrying works by slots. Carrying large things slows you down.

DECIDEDThe belt can be crafted in several ways. Backpacks have unique functions: for example, the firewood one is a frame of sticks shaped like a double L for carrying firewood on your back. At the base you leave some and pick up others, or none if you want to travel light.

DECIDEDClothing and armour matter for combat.

DECIDEDThe inventory is a panel with the slots of each body location, and the slots depend on the equipment you wear.

Base character slots

PROPOSAL

  • Body (clothing and armour) in layers: inner layer, warmth or protection layer, outer layer; plus head, hands and feet.

METHOD

Layered clothing on the character

DECIDEDClothing and armour are seen worn, in layers: shirt (and hose) always; over them a tunic or dress; gambeson; mail; surcoat. Whatever is covered is not drawn.

METHOD

  • Clothing textures are grey and the colour is kept separately, to dye without rebuilding the model.

9. Progression and technologies

DECIDEDCharacter technologies, unlocked through stations:

  1. Building the station gives its basic recipes.
  2. Practising at it opens the next ones.
  3. Some recipes let you build new stations (that is how you advance from the Early to the Late Middle Ages).
  4. Whoever knows a recipe can write it into a tome to teach it to others.

DECIDEDNo forced specialisations; the limit is play time.

DECIDEDDurability on tools, weapons and armour. They are repaired at the station that made them, with material of the same type. No quality tiers for now.

10. Building

DECIDEDIt is the first thing to be developed.

DECIDEDFree placement that turns into a grid with the first structural piece; free decoration.

DECIDEDIf it cannot bear the weight, you cannot build on top. If a piece holding up others is dismantled or broken in a siege, what is above is destroyed.

DECIDEDDismantling recovers 75% of the material.

DECIDEDPalisades almost impossible to bring down without a siege. Stone is almost impossible to break: in a siege of a castle, gates and wooden elements break, not the walls.

DECIDEDFire damages wood with temporary damage over time, and it is visible.

DECIDEDBuildings decay. Decay is prevented by depositing materials in the boundary stone, in a low amount so as not to force farming.

METHOD
  • Grid anchored per building; support graph for load.
  • Before dismantling a support, what would fall is highlighted in red and confirmation is asked for.
  • Resistance by material and damage type (blow, siege, fire).
  • Upkeep: the boundary stone slowly consumes the deposited materials (wood for wooden parts, straw for roofs, lime for stone). While there is some, nothing decays; if it runs out, slow decay begins, with warnings.

Building works: frame, materials and time

DECIDEDBuilding is a work in progress: you place the frame of the piece, bring it the materials and press build; it takes time depending on the piece. If others help, it takes less.

DECIDEDEach material has a weight class, and height matters: the material has to be brought to where the work is.

DECIDEDBalance: alone or as a couple you can easily build a one-floor log cabin, and a second wooden floor with a pulley. A group of friends can raise stone walls and towers, with a crane and people split between pulling, guiding and placing.

METHOD
  • The half-built work looks like a framework; it keeps the delivered materials and the work done (in the pure logic, with the author of each delivery and each stint of work).
  • Reference figures (human power): one person pulling about 50 kg lifts about 150–250 kg with a block of 3 or 5 pulleys, and 750–1,500 kg with a windlass; one or two people fit in a treadwheel crane.

DECIDEDEnd of decay: stone remains as a ruin indefinitely, gradually covered by plants and roots. Wood rots and disappears.

DECIDEDRuins belong to nobody. They can be cleared, but they give no stone back: it is no longer dressed stone but rubble. Clearing them takes time.

11. Lands and boundary stones

DECIDEDLand is claimed with a boundary stone placed in person. You can claim a mountain pass, a bridge or a ford and charge a toll: whoever does not want to pay can find another way.

DECIDEDNo waiting period for new accounts: everyone must be able to settle soon.

DECIDEDExclusion radius: if a house is settled within a certain distance, you cannot settle there, even if your plot is small. The radius grows with the boundary stone's level. People from the same family can settle inside the radius if the boundary stone allows it in its options.

DECIDEDConquest: you conquer by destroying the enemy's boundary stones. If you have a high-level territory, its surroundings count as your land, so when the nearby enemy boundary stones are removed that land becomes yours.

DECIDEDBoundary stone level: it rises by depositing materials of the corresponding technology level. The higher the level, the larger the exclusion radius and the territory.

DECIDEDAccess to doors and chests through family permissions.

PROPOSAL Rules kept from v0.3:
  • Land grows with what is built on it, not with the number of accounts.
  • Land with no activity or upkeep eventually becomes free.
  • Limit on claims per character.
  • Important veins cannot be hoarded entirely.
  • No claims near the starting areas.
METHOD

The boundary stone is the key piece of each land: it can be attacked with siege equipment only when offline protection is not active or war has been declared.

DECIDEDWhen a boundary stone falls, its buildings pass to the conqueror if they lie within their territory; if not, they remain ownerless until someone places their own boundary stone.

12. Offline raiding and war

DECIDEDOffline protection: if no family member is online, their buildings cannot be damaged. It activates a few minutes after the last one logs out and does not activate if the base is under attack.

DECIDEDException: if war has been declared between the two families, a siege is allowed even while they are offline.

DECIDEDThe problem to solve: declaring war must not become the easy way to raid offline.

PROPOSAL How a war is declared:
  • Only with a cause: non-payment of a vassalage (what you already decided) or a war accepted by both sides. Without a cause, there is no war and offline protection stays.
  • Advance notice: the war starts some time after it is declared (for example, 24 real hours), and the attacked family is notified. This gives time to log in, pay what is owed or prepare the defence.
  • Paying what is owed stops the war before it starts.
  • Limited duration and, when it ends, a period during which war cannot be declared again on the same family.

That way, war only exists when someone has accepted a vassalage and not paid it, or when both want to fight.

DECIDEDWar rules approved: war requires a casus belli.

13. Combat

DECIDEDNo health bars; direct and skill-based. PvP on; off within the family.

DECIDEDMounted and ranged combat later, when their turn comes in development.

METHOD
  • Localised wounds, bleeding and fatigue, visible on the character and in the warnings.
  • Armour by zone and type of blow.
  • Healing by wound type (analysis, section 15).
  • Strikes in five directions with the new character: we will test moving the arm towards the target (inverse kinematics) over a base animation, because the animation pack only has a few strikes.

DECIDEDFencing with a strike rose (combat v2): over the opponent, five zones (high, left, right, low and thrust). Defence in three layers: a parry without side that tires you, a perfect parry at the last moment, and a master strike from the same side. Feints and stamina. Guards remain as an advanced mode.

DECIDEDShield (provisional): it also stops arrows and stones from the front.

DECIDEDTouch camera: dragging a finger on a free area turns the camera, tapping without dragging strikes; with the bow, the camera follows where you aim.

DECIDEDHealing: the analysis table is the base (bandaging, stitching, cauterizing, splinting, herbs, rest). It can be done mid-fight, but slowly and leaving you exposed.

DECIDEDFriendly fire: follows PvP. A strike does nothing to a member of your family; anyone else is wounded like a normal hit.

16. Economy and NPCs

DECIDEDCoins are minted only by NPCs. They are earned through NPC orders: they ask for what they need and you bring it; they do not buy whatever you want to sell.

DECIDEDMarket stalls that work while you are offline.

DECIDEDNPCs in your service, like the mercenaries of the era: you have to get them the gear they will wear, their food and their pay; you keep them as long as you can support them. Comfort affects these NPCs. Sentries work this way.

DECIDEDNo NPC villages or bandits at first.

PROPOSAL
  • Between players: barter or coin, as they wish.
  • NPC traders are in few places in each country; their orders rotate and ask for things from the area or from other bands, to give reasons to travel.
  • A hired NPC eats and is paid at regular intervals.

DECIDEDIf not paid, the NPC leaves and takes the gear.

DECIDEDThe NPCs for hire are travelling merchants and mercenaries who arrive at your town or settlement.

APPROVED How they arrive and what types there are:
  • Arrival: every so often a traveller passes through your settlement (you need a boundary stone for them to come). They stay for a while; if you do not hire them, they move on. With a higher boundary stone level they come more often and in more types.
  • Also in NPC towns: there is always someone available there, in exchange for travelling to get them.
  • Types:
    • Mercenary / sentry: guards and defends your lands.
    • Woodcutter: fells trees and stacks firewood on your land.
    • Day labourer: sows, waters and harvests.
    • Herder: looks after, feeds and takes your animals out to graze.
    • Messenger: carries your letters.
    • Travelling merchant: not hired; brings orders and the odd product from another region.
  • Each one needs their tools or weapons (you provide them), food and pay in coin. With comfort (bed, roof, better food) they work better or ask for less.

17. Families, alliances and vassalages

DECIDEDFamilies, with PvP off inside them.

DECIDEDVassalage: an alliance that requires a periodic payment. If it is not paid on time, the lord has the right to declare war.

DECIDEDAlliances: PvP between allies is turned off only if both sides enable it.

DECIDEDProximity-only communication.

DECIDEDCoat of arms editor of its own for each family.

DECIDEDVassalage is paid in coin.

DECIDEDLetters by NPC messenger: you send a messenger to a town or settlement you know; they travel there and deliver the letter to a member of your family who is at that place.

METHOD
  • You write the letter (with parchment and ink, or on simple paper at first) and give it to the messenger with their pay.
  • The messenger really covers the distance, at the speed of a traveller on foot or on horseback; the letter takes as long as it would take.
  • On arrival, they leave it at the settlement's boundary stone or mailbox, and any family member passing by picks it up.
  • You can only send to places your character has already visited.

DECIDEDMessengers cannot be intercepted or killed.

18. Technical architecture

METHOD
  • A single online world; playing solo also needs a connection.
  • The first milestones are prototyped in the browser without a server; the logic is written so the server can run it later without rewriting it.
  • Authoritative server over websockets; database with accounts, characters and world changes.
  • Versioned saves so progress is not lost with each update.
  • Tests before each delivery: headless browser, real screenshots; online, several clients and bots with simulated lag.
  • Deployment: GitHub → host, to be chosen when we get to online.
  • Every action that changes the world is a message with an author (who, what, where) that the pure logic validates and applies; it is kept in a log. That way the server will be able to validate, log and replay it without rewriting anything.
  • A single scene and a single character loader for every part of the game (building, combat, character).
  • The world is loaded and unloaded in chunks around the player from the first terrain.
