1. Vision, pillars and era

DECIDEDMedieval survival and building game, 100% in the browser. A single online world: play alone, as a pair or in a group within the same world, with PvP.

DECIDEDAn era with progression: you start in the Early Middle Ages and technology advances up to the Late Middle Ages. No fantasy for now.

Pillars

  • Realistic mechanics. What you would do in real life costs what it would cost.
  • The world is huge and travel matters.
  • From survival to kingdom, or to living well in isolation.
  • Whoever plays alone has a complete game, in the same online world as everyone else.
  • Everything rests on knowledge, skill and equipment, not on character levels or stats.
  • Skill-based combat, without health bars.
  • What is yours is safe while you are away.

2. Platform, camera and product

DECIDEDOwn website with accounts. Paid server if needed. PC, mobile and tablet.

DECIDEDAlways online (valravnx, 30 Oct): the game is always played connected to the server, also from the website, and that is the only mode; there is no separate offline or solo mode. Whoever plays alone does so in everyone's world. Everything that changes the world goes through the server with its author, and what one player sees the others see too.

DECIDEDSemi-isometric third person with free rotation.

DECIDEDLanguages: English, Spanish and Russian.

DECIDEDFree forever; what is paid is only appearance, in recipes (see section 20). You see exactly what you buy before buying it. No loot boxes or wheels, no premium currency, no season pass and no rush offers to push people to buy (4 Nov).

DECIDEDMandatory functional parity (4 Nov): nothing paid gives a functional, defensive, economic or tactical advantage. A paid version has exactly the same as its unpaid equivalent: resistance, stats, collisions, what it blocks and what it lets through, and how it is placed, used and broken. What has no equivalent is only decoration: it doesn't stop projectiles, give cover, close off passages or get in the way of walking.

DECIDEDOptional tutorial always available in the pause menu: you can read about every system in the game.

DECIDEDCheaters: immediate ban, also by IP.

DECIDEDMinimum requirements: to be decided midway through development.

METHOD

  • Game texts in translation files from the first milestone, so adding languages does not require touching the code.
  • IP bans: note that an IP is sometimes shared by several people (a household, a residence hall, some mobile carriers) and that a VPN gets around it. Best combined with banning the account and its email.

DECIDEDName: Kinhold. Domain: playkinhold.com (1 Nov). Trademark registration still pending.

3. Visual style and sound

DECIDEDCode-generated low-poly 3D, with your reviews to fine-tune it.

DECIDEDSound and music that are relaxing and not harsh.

DECIDEDWorld style: «miniature realism» (style test v5): clean shapes, textures made by code, soft light with contact shadows, an uncropped world.

DECIDEDArt direction (4 Nov), that of style test v5 for everything visible: simple geometry and readable silhouettes, with believable medieval proportions; the richness goes into materials, light and post-processing, not extra polygons. Neither hyperrealism nor caricature. The same hand in architecture, terrain, objects and characters.

DECIDEDAtmosphere: careful sunlight and ambient light, measured dynamic shadows, materials that respond to light (no flat, poor surfaces), a soft glow on lights, restrained post-processing and fog for depth. First an attractive, coherent image; costly effects later or never.

DECIDEDCharacters: never sculpted or invented by code. The body comes from the free MakeHuman mesh (CC0) and in the own workshop its clothing, hair, beard, skin and eyes are made by code. The animations are Quaternius's (CC0), transferred to that skeleton.

METHOD
  • Models defined by parameters (proportions, colours, sides): your adjustments are applied by changing numbers.
  • Screenshots of every new model in each delivery for you to review.
  • One palette per material so everything is consistent.
  • Own headless workshop (Blender + MPFB2, with pinned versions): generates the dressed characters, reduces meshes and transfers animations from one skeleton to another.
  • Levels of detail: simpler versions of characters and pieces to see them from afar and cope with many people on mobile.
  • Quality that adapts to the device (browser, PC and Android): graphics profiles and settings; levels of detail and instancing; few dynamic lights, controlled shadows and draw distance; no advanced global illumination or costly volumetric effects.
  • Six graphics profiles (valravnx, 4 Nov), from low-end phone to high-end PC: Basic phone, Mid-range phone, High-end phone and tablet, Basic PC, Mid-range PC and High-end PC. They change the drawing resolution, the shadows, the ambient occlusion, the glow of lights, the light shafts and the anti-aliasing; the near fog and what is drawn nearby are the same in all six (nothing that matters for play). Horizon (valravnx, 5 Nov: «greatly increase the map draw distance, even without detail, to see the mountains and the shapes»): High-end phone and the three PC profiles see, behind the nearby terrain, the whole island in coarse form (the relief and the colour of the landscape, every 200 m in High-end phone, Basic PC and Mid-range PC, every 100 in High-end PC) and the sea up to its edge, blended with the sky by the haze; only the distant landscape: no players, no buildings, no individual trees. The forest from afar (valravnx, 5 Nov: «so that at any distance you can tell the biomes apart quite well»): where there is forest, a canopy layer at the height of the trees, with bumps and crown patches, darker the farther away (the shade inside the forest); on PC, also from where the real trees end (200 m) to the horizon. The starting one comes from the device and is refined by measuring (post-processing test v1, with "Measure").
  • The style's post-processing (valravnx, 4 Nov): ambient occlusion (corners, joints and contacts), tone mapping (light to shadow transition), colour grading (colour identity: slightly cool shadows, slightly warm highlights, less garish greens), moderate glow of lights, fog (depth), blur only in specific scenes and optional light shafts. In three.js, with the postprocessing (everything in one pass) and n8ao (ambient occlusion) libraries.
  • Indicative targets (4 Nov): 30 frames per second on a mid-range Android and 60 on PC. They are targets, not something already measured.
  • No graphics setting may hide or change what matters for playing: what can be used and what happens in a fight read the same in any profile.

4. World and size

DECIDEDAn invented pseudo-Europe, 5 regions with their own style (architecture, clothing, names). Biomes larger than 100 km² in northern, temperate and southern bands. A coherent sea.

DECIDEDA 2 h day and a 1 h night. One season per real month.

DECIDEDEach region has its own resources to trade, but none are essential.

Size calculation

Assumptions: walking at about 5 km/h, a horse at a sustained trot at about 12 km/h, and the sea adds around 40% to the side of the map. One day-night cycle is 3 real hours.

DECIDEDA world of 256 × 256 km including the sea (chosen by valravnx on 27 Oct, with seed 66172, in the map sketches): mainland regions of about 75 × 75 km and an island region of about 2,000 km², separated by sea. Crossing a mainland region takes about 15 hours on foot or 6 on horseback.

DECIDEDNames (chosen by valravnx on 28 Oct): the continent is Kinland, and its five regions are Whitholm (the northeastern island: cold, rainy and limestone), Eikwold (northwest: forests, plains and coast), Rudmere (southwest: the warmest, holm oak woods on red sandstone, with the lake), Highfell (the central massif, with the highest peak) and Storaa (southeast: the driest, where the great river runs). They are regions, not countries: countries, if they ever come, will be made by the players.

DECIDEDPlaytest world: Whitholm (valravnx, 29 Oct): while testing with other people, the game is played only on the island (about 2,000 km²). The rest of the world exists, but a sea more than 20 km wide separates it and cannot be crossed by swimming. It will be opened later: if there are enough players, or by letting them discover it themselves when ships arrive. The island is divided into districts. Where you appear (valravnx, 30 Oct): each new player, at a random spot in three starting districts (Motdale, Breiddal and Strandvang, the vales and meadows of the east and south-east), always in ordinary biomes (never in the mountains, alpine pasture or snow) and on flat ground away from rivers. Its resources are discussed separately. Only at a free spot (valravnx, 5 Nov: «you appear at a random FREE point; if a zone has an active boundary stone, it stops being a spawn»): the random start only chooses among sites whose zone has no boundary stone. The districts (valravnx, 30 Oct) are eight, each one or several whole river basins: Breiddal (the great south-eastern vale), Motdale (the eastern valley, where the rivers meet), Nordwold (the north), Lindholt (the north-west), Strandvang (the east coast), Furulid (the western slope of the sierra), Sudholt (the south) and Ravnsnes (the western peninsula). Each district is divided into zones of roughly 1 km² for clans (section 11).

METHOD

Since the world comes from a seed, it can be expanded at the edges later without touching what already exists. With few people at first, the medium world may feel empty: we will keep an eye on it once there are players.

Relief, rivers and forests at real scale

DECIDEDEverything at real scale, like a world still unexplored by man. There are plains, but the world does not feel like a giant flat: mountain ranges like the Pyrenees, with peaks of about 3,000 m, valleys about 1,000 m deep and passes to cross them; the plains lie in the low basins. From anywhere you can see relief on the horizon.

DECIDEDRivers of three sizes, depending on the water they gather: streams 1 to 3 m wide, which can be forded; rivers 10 to 30 m wide, with few fords; great rivers 50 to 150 m wide, which need a boat or a bridge.

DECIDEDTruly dense forests, with clearings. No paths at first: they form through use.

DECIDEDRelief matters: steep slopes slow you down when walking, carts cannot climb steep hills and on a hillside you have to level the ground to lay foundations.

DECIDEDSettling makes sense in specific places without forcing anyone: by the river there is water for thirst, fertile soil in the floodplain, current for the mill, and fords and bridges that can be controlled (and tolled).

METHOD
  • Water first, then the terrain (like the most believable terrain generators, based on hydrology): the mountain ranges and the river network flowing from them to the sea are laid out first, like a tree; the terrain is carved from that network, so valleys appear where water runs and passes where two valleys meet at the top.
  • Each river's width comes from the water it gathers (how many tributaries reach it).
  • Forests with repeated single-mesh trees and simple versions at a distance, in chunks around the player, so they hold up on mobile.

Water and swimming

DECIDEDAt fords you cross on foot. In deep water you swim slowly and it tires you a lot. In great rivers, the current drags you downstream.

DECIDEDStamina and drowning (valravnx, 30 Oct): a single stamina, the same as when running with Shift. Swimming drains it, faster the heavier what you carry (clothes, armour and load): lightly dressed you last a good while; in mail and a gambeson, or carrying a log, it runs out almost at once. Without stamina you drown and die. You can go into the water in armour, but you drown sooner (it used to say you could not swim in armour).

DECIDEDCarts only cross at fords and bridges. That way fords and bridges are truly valuable.

METHOD
  • Continent outline first; climate by latitude and sea; large biomes with gradual transitions; natural borders.
  • Seed-based generation, in chunks, saving only the changes.

5. Travel, map and sailing

DECIDEDWhitholm is the king's island, and it is mapped (valravnx, 31 Oct): when you arrive you are given the king's map, with the coast, the districts, the rivers and the city of Strandvang; nobody gets lost on the island. The continent (Kinland) is unexplored: there is no map of it, it is reached by ship and it has to be mapped. Endgame cartography: a cartographer's table to draw on paper what has been explored of the continent; the map is an item that can be copied, sold or stolen from you.

DECIDEDPaths form through use. Buildable bridges in various materials and shapes.

DECIDEDSailing in a late phase: design ships and sail with others.

METHOD
  • Each ground tile stores how much it is walked on: trail, then road; without use, the grass returns.
  • Bridges and ships use the building system; ships with buoyancy rules.

6. Character, death and logging out

DECIDEDCreation: a few appearance settings, man or woman, and starting region. You start at a random accessible spot in the chosen region (never in the sea or on a peak).

DECIDEDNeeds: hunger, thirst, cold, heat and fatigue, with warnings in circles on the right; tapping them shows the effect and information.

DECIDEDYou die of thirst and hunger (valravnx, 7 Nov: «it's not survival if you don't have to survive»): with thirst or hunger at maximum you start to lose blood little by little (the same blood as in combat, no health bars) and, if you do not drink or eat, after a while you die, with the usual death (your bound bed and half of what you carried, lootable). Thirst kills before hunger. To tune by playing: about 20 real minutes at full thirst and about 40 at full hunger.

DECIDEDLater on (an expansion after the first version of the survival milestone, not before): five more states, with their warning in the same circles. Wet (rain, swimming or crossing rivers; adds to cold), infected wound (if it isn't cleaned or stitched properly), spoiled food (raw or rotten meat; makes you ill), pain (after a fight or a fall, clumsier until healed) and drunk (beer and wine, with a downside in combat). Boredom, sadness or panic are not included: with the character asleep while offline they would penalise being away, and panic would go against skill-based combat.

DECIDEDThe character does not improve through levels or stats: everything depends on the player's knowledge, skill and equipment.

DECIDEDDeath: you respawn in your bound bed; without a bed, where you started the game. On death, roughly half of what you carry can be looted and the other half breaks. Permadeath is left for a future version.

DECIDEDLogging out: the character falls asleep in the world, goes to their bed automatically if not already in it, and shows a disconnected symbol above them.

DECIDEDWhat breaks on death is always chosen at random, to prevent exploits.

  • If you were in combat in the last minute before logging out, the vulnerable time is longer (for example, 15 minutes).
  • The times are numbers to tune when we test.

8. Gathering and resources

DECIDEDTrees fall, are transported and are cut; they grow back in real weeks and can be planted.

DECIDEDOre veins do not regenerate, but the world has plenty of material: it is not a scarce resource.

DECIDEDThe ordinary near everyone; the great, few and marked: there are small iron and coal veins all over the world (one of each in every area of about 14 km), so nobody has to travel far for everyday needs. There are also a few great veins, marked on the map, to exploit to the full. Whitholm, the playtest island, has its own great iron vein high in the sierra (valravnx, 30 Oct).

DECIDEDYoung and mature trees, clearly different (review of 1 Nov): young trees are 3 to 5 m tall, slender and with a sparse crown, and grow mostly at forest edges, in clearings and in regrowth; with a stone axe they give a thin log and rods, the timber of the first house. Mature trees are 30% taller and thicker than before; with a stone axe they may still take a long time.

DECIDEDThe tree falls by physics, not by animation (review of 1 Nov): it pivots on the stump towards the notch cut into it (without one, towards where it leans or downhill), creaks before falling as a warning and injures whoever is underneath; the server decides the damage, with its author (milestone “Real felling”).

DECIDEDScrub and bushes can be cleared (valravnx, 8 Nov): with the stone axe (or with the flake, more slowly) the scrub and bushes of the world are cleared in a few blows; they give branches and the odd rod, and grow back in a season. The berry bush too: any berries it had are lost.

DECIDEDLoose rocks can be broken (valravnx, 8 Nov: «imagine building a castle running around searching for little pebbles on the ground»): with the stone maul the medium rocks of the world (up to about 1 m) are broken, with many blows depending on their size; it tires you, wears the maul and gives a heap of loose stone (more the bigger it is). The rock disappears and comes back after a few real months. Big rocks and outcrops, only with iron (pick, wedges and maul): the quarry of the systems document (N2), which gives rough blocks.

Proposed gatherables: section 3 of the analysis document.

Digging and mining

DECIDEDYou can dig the ground and open mines. Outside: trenches, moats and levelling for foundations with a round brush that lowers or smooths with soft edges. Inside the rock, each pick stroke removes a rounded handful of rock in the direction you face: tunnels go straight, uphill or curving.

DECIDEDNo grid look: inside, the rock is split into small cubes of about 50 cm, but the wall is drawn as a smooth surface wrapping them, with the rock's roughness taken from a fixed seed by position: everyone sees the same irregular cave.

DECIDEDWhen you enter a mine, what is above is hidden (like the roof of houses) and what the character cannot see fades to black.

DECIDEDSupport: no support, no progress. Each material holds a maximum span of hollow roof to the nearest support (undug rock or a timber prop): earth 1 m, clay or gravel 1.5 m, soft rock 2.5 m, hard rock 4 m (values to tune by playing). If a stroke would leave more loose roof than it holds, it does not happen and a warning to shore it up appears: there are no collapses. The deeper, the shorter the span: a deep mine needs props every little way.

DECIDEDDownwards: in earth, a vertical wall reaches 1.5 m at most; to go deeper the sides must be stepped (slope) or the shaft shored up. In rock you can go straight down, with the same span limit.

DECIDEDTimber props (posts and a log crossbeam) are placed like building works: frame, material and time, and several people finish sooner. Each material takes more or fewer strokes depending on its hardness and the tool. A decent mine costs time or people.

DECIDEDSpoil is carried out in a basket or wheelbarrow, and it is material that gets used. What each one is for (earth to fill and level, stone to build…) is decided with the recipes.

DECIDEDRealistic layers and veins: topsoil, clay or gravel beneath, and then each region's bedrock (limestone, sandstone, granite, slate). Surface iron is scarce and poor, and is found by signs (reddish water, waterlogged ground); the good iron is in veins inside the rock, which need a mine. Coal crops out on some slopes and is dug in shallow pits. Veins come from the world seed.

DECIDEDNo digging on someone else's land (their zone, section 11) unless its owner has invited you. The king's zones (valravnx, 4 Nov): zones holding a vein marked on the map, the common starting point or the city cannot be claimed (those of the random starting sites can, like any other: 5 Nov); anyone digs and gathers there. Small unmarked veins can end up inside private land (its owner and guests dig them): there is one of each per area of some 14 km, so nobody lacks one nearby.

SUPERSEDED · 4 Novnor within its exclusion radius (there is no radius any more: the land is the whole zone)

METHOD
  • «Smooth voxels»: the server only stores which small cubes were removed in each 8 × 8 × 8 m chunk of the world (a few hundred bytes per dug chunk; nothing where nobody has dug), each stroke with its author. Each client only rebuilds the mesh of the touched chunk.
  • What is seen inside the mine: from the character's cell the open space and what its torch lights are traversed; the rest stays black. The server only sends the hollows the player has seen or has in front, so nobody sees other people's mines and veins through the rock.
  • The whole world can be dug: relief and materials come from the seed in the same small cubes, and only changes are stored. Near the player the smoothed mesh is drawn, and far away the normal relief with levels of detail (the radius is set by measuring on mobile). The mesh is rebuilt in a separate thread so strokes do not stutter.
  • The span is checked in the pure logic with every stroke: from the new hollow, the nearest roof support is searched for. The server does the same calculation.
  • Performance test done (digging v1): on valravnx's phone, 70 fps and 24 ms per stroke on average (52 ms worst).

9. Progression and technologies

SUPERSEDED · 1 NovCharacter technologies, unlocked through stations: Building the station gives its basic recipes. Practising at it opens the next ones. Some recipes let you build new stations (that is how you advance from the Early to the Late Middle Ages). Whoever knows a recipe can write it into a tome to teach it to others.

DECIDEDNo learning recipes (review of 1 Nov): every recipe is always visible in the crafting panel (what you can make now at the top, what you almost can below it with what is missing in red, and greyed out what opens later). What is made by hand is crafted anywhere, with what you carry and what lies on the ground next to you. Stations are not levels but tools by use (the campfire cooks, the log bench works wood, the anvil works iron): a recipe asks for the station it really needs, and you advance from the Early to the Late Middle Ages by building the stations that earlier recipes allow. Recipe tomes are dropped.

DECIDEDA station’s recipes use what is placed on it (valravnx, 6 Nov): each station (the knapping stone, the log bench, the campfire…) has somewhere to leave materials, on top of the table or beside it, and they show there. Its recipes use what has been placed on it, not what is on the ground, and whoever crafts has to stand in front of it.

DECIDEDWorkstations are built as a building project (valravnx, 6 Nov): from their own tab of the build menu, «Workstations», apart from furniture. You place the frame and bring it the materials, like any other build; they are not crafted and dropped on the ground. Each one goes where it belongs:

10. Building

DECIDEDIt is the first thing to be developed.

DECIDEDFree placement that turns into a grid with the first structural piece; free decoration.

DECIDEDIf it cannot bear the weight, you cannot build on top. If a piece holding up others is dismantled or broken in a siege, what is above is destroyed.

DECIDEDDismantling recovers 75% of the material.

DECIDEDPalisades almost impossible to bring down without a siege. Stone is almost impossible to break: in a siege of a castle, gates and wooden elements break, not the walls.

DECIDEDFire damages wood with temporary damage over time, and it is visible.

DECIDEDBuildings decay. Decay is prevented by depositing materials in the boundary stone, in a low amount so as not to force farming.

METHOD
  • Grid anchored per building; support graph for load.
  • Before dismantling a support, what would fall is highlighted in red and confirmation is asked for.
  • Resistance by material and damage type (blow, siege, fire).
  • Upkeep: the boundary stone slowly consumes the deposited materials (wood for wooden parts, straw for roofs, lime for stone). While there is some, nothing decays; if it runs out, slow decay begins, with warnings.

Building works: frame, materials and time

11. Lands and boundary stones

DECIDEDYour land is the whole zone (valravnx, 4 Nov): it is claimed with one boundary stone per character, placed in person (on the mainland up to three more in total, once you get there by boat; valravnx, 31 Oct), and your land is the whole zone of the district where you place it (about 1.2 km²): one zone, one owner. DECIDEDConquest: you conquer by toppling the enemy's boundary stone (with siege gear, at war or while their offline protection is not active). SUPERSEDED · 4 NovConquest: you conquer by destroying the enemy's boundary stones. If you have a high-level territory, its surroundings count as your land, so when the nearby enemy boundary stones are removed that land becomes yours.

Districts, zones and clans

DECIDEDSeparate from the boundary stone (valravnx, 30 Oct): the boundary stone is still what covers your home and what you build (upkeep, protection, exclusion radius). Clan matters go separately, with the districts and their zones. Here “clan” means the family of section 17.

DECIDEDZones: every district is divided into fixed zones of roughly 1 km² (room for a castle), with natural, curved borders. Any clan can take a zone by raising its castle and placing in it the centre, a piece of its own, different from the boundary stone. The zone shows on the map with its banner and its name. If they look too small on the map, they are made bigger. A clan only takes free zones (valravnx, 4 Nov): if the zone is already a player's land, only by toppling their stone at war; or the owner joins the clan and brings it in.

DECIDEDTaking a whole district: the centre is also what gives the district. The clan that holds it is its lord, and the district shows on the map with its banner. The castle can go anywhere in the district.

DECIDEDVassals: a clan that takes a zone in a district that already has a lord becomes a vassal of the main clan. It still rules its zone (its castle, its doors and its permissions).

DECIDEDRent: the work done in the district gives the lord rent, without taking anything from whoever does it: whoever fells a hundred logs keeps their hundred, as anywhere else. The rent is not items (logs have to be processed and carried): it arrives as coin at the centre, and the clan spends it with NPCs (mercenaries, wandering traders, upkeep…). That way the lord is better off with many people working in the district, small groups included, instead of harassing them.

DECIDEDProtecting pays off: if the lord clan attacks or kills someone working in its district, that person's rent stops counting for a while. It can attack a vassal clan (for instance, to drive out one it believes is going to take the district), but then it loses the rent of that whole clan for a while.

DECIDEDSiege window: the centre can only be besieged in its window. Between one window and the next it keeps collecting rent.

PROPOSAL Still to be set (from the chat, 30 Oct):

  • The rent cut for attacking lasts one real week.
  • The window: once a week, about 2 hours, at a time the clan chooses.
  • When the lord's centre falls, the district passes to the clan that brought it down if it has its centre there (a rebelling vassal, for instance); if not, it becomes free and the first clan to place its own takes it. Vassals keep their zones and pass to the new lord.

  • Abandonment: a centre without upkeep ends up freeing its zone and, if it is the lord's, the district.
METHOD The rules carried over from v0.3 were settled on 4 Nov: land does not grow with what is built (it is the whole zone); unused land returns to the king (above); one boundary stone per character (31 Oct); marked veins lie in the king's zones (section 8); and the starting areas can be claimed as personal land.
METHOD

DECIDEDOffline protection: if no family member is online, their buildings cannot be damaged. It activates a few minutes after the last one logs out and does not activate if the base is under attack.

DECIDEDException: if war has been declared between the two families, a siege is allowed even while they are offline.

DECIDEDThe problem to solve: declaring war must not become the easy way to raid offline.

PROPOSAL How a war is declared:
  • Only with a cause: non-payment of a vassalage (what you already decided) or a war accepted by both sides. Without a cause, there is no war and offline protection stays. SUPERSEDED · 30 Oct Non-payment no longer exists with district vassalage (the rent reaches the centre by itself): which causes remain is the question in section 21, for the meeting after P3.
  • Advance notice: the war starts some time after it is declared (for example, 24 real hours), and the attacked family is notified. This gives time to log in, pay what is owed or prepare the defence.
  • Paying what is owed stops the war before it starts.
  • Limited duration and, when it ends, a period during which war cannot be declared again on the same family.

That way, war only exists when someone has accepted a vassalage and not paid it, or when both want to fight.

18. Technical architecture

METHOD
  • A single online world; playing solo also needs a connection.
  • The first milestones are prototyped in the browser without a server; since 30 Oct, everything is made and tested online; the logic is written so the server can run it later without rewriting it.
  • Authoritative server over websockets; database with accounts, characters and world changes.
  • Versioned saves so progress is not lost with each update.
  • Tests before each delivery: headless browser, real screenshots; online, several clients and bots with simulated lag.
  • Deployment: GitHub → host, to be chosen when we get to online.
  • Every action that changes the world is a message with an author (who, what, where) that the pure logic validates and applies; it is kept in a log. That way the server will be able to validate, log and replay it without rewriting anything.
  • A single scene and a single character loader for every part of the game (building, combat, character).
  • The world is loaded and unloaded in chunks around the player from the first terrain.
